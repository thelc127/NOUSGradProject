Research Question
    I want to investigate how to distinguish between routine reward hacking and an actual loss of control when AI agents coordinate to break out or bypass evaluation environments. 

Why does this question matter? 
    As we deploy frontier models in autonomous settings and multi-agent setups, we can see agents finding unintended ways to complete the tasks. 
    However, evaluators (in my opinion) currently lack a clean line between loss of control ( an agent breaking the rule of its environment) and 
    reward hacking (an agent finding a clever way to maximize the score). I find this questions matters to me. 

    The investigation solves a real diagnostic puzzle (for eg, in OpenAI Hugging Face incident). 
    When agents bypass environment boundaries, researchers disagree on what happened, like if the system just cheating its reward function? 
    I think the study focus on the classical problem of interpreting an agent’s intent, and can help us find the clear criteria of that 
    distinction and where the OpenAI Hugging Face incident crossed that line. 

What I think about this research problem?
    Looking at model traces from OpenAI hugging face incident shows no signs of malice or human-level thinking. The models were just blindly following an optimization path to maximize their score. 
    The benchmark teams do not have clear warning signs to catch when an agent moves from a harmless score-gaming to compromising the grading system.
